help

  1. Click the "add a character" button and select 1 or more images.
    You can do this as many times as needed until all subjects you want to compare are on the page.

    Note: Duplicate subjects are allowed. If you are trying to add a file with the same name as the previous one, your device may decide "that's the same file, I don't think you meant to do that" and refuse. If this happens, simply choose a different file, remove it, and add the first one again. Machines can be silly sometimes.

    Note 2: This website is entirely static. Your files will not be sent to a server; everything to do with your images is processed exclusively on your device. Once your browser has loaded the page, it does not require internet to work. If you don't believe me: this page is a single HTML file, you can save it (with ctrl+s or the right click menu) and use it offline. The page icon might not work though...

  2. Select the measurement area.
    Drag the "top of measurement" slider to the left until the top line is at the top of the subject (typically the top of the head for humanoids/creatures).
    Drag the "bottom of measurement" slider to the right until the bottom line is at the bottom of the subject (typically the heels for humanoids/creatures).

    Note: the measurement does not have to be from heel to head. The "bottom of measurement" just needs to be the part of the subject that should be on the floor, and the "top of measurement" should be the a point on the subject with a visible, straight, vertical distance from the "bottom of measurement". That distance must be indicated by the height.

    If the top/bottom of the measurement for the subject is not visible on the image, use the top/bottom extension.
    1 extension = 1x the height of the image added to the upper/lower bound of the "top/bottom of measurement" sliders. More specifically, "top extension"=1 will add (upward) 1 image height to the upper bound, and "bottom extension"=1 will add (downward) 1 image height to the lower bound. Extensions can be increased until you feel there is sufficient room to measure the subject.

    This is useful when the image you want to use is not a fullbody.

  3. Measure your subjects.
    If you know the height of your subject--or at least the part in the measurement area--input it to the "height" textbox. I programmed it to be quite flexible, so you don't have to worry about conversions unless you have a particularly unusual unit. If you have any suggestions, please let me know.

    If you don't know the height of your subject, but you have another subject you do know the height of, and both have the same scale, (i.e. 1px in subject A = 1px in subject B; for example, both are from the same game and are screenshotted at the same level of zoom) put the unknown subject's height as "px" and make sure both subjects are in the same "pixel group".

    Pixel groups will use the average "local scale" (meters per pixel) of members with known height to calculate the heights of members with unknown ("px") heights. The calculated height will appear on the "output height" and will be automatically used to scale them as though their height is known. Use this feature if you have an in-game screenshot of a standardized bottle of diet coke and want to use it to determine how tall the protagonist is.

  4. Organize your subjects.

    • Left/right
      Use the "horizontal offset" slider to move subjects around to the left or right on the canvas. The "horizontal offset" slider works in meters and has a range equal to the width of all of your subjects standing side by side.

      Changing the height of a subject may impact where they are positioned horizontally, as it will change how wide they are.

    • Front/back
      If a subject is in front of another subject, use the "order" input to change what order they are shown in. Subjects with an "order" of 0 will be displayed on the canvas in the order (back to front) they appear on the input (which is determined by the order they were added with the "add a character" button).

      A higher "order" number will move the subject forward (in front of others), while a lower "order" number will move them backward (behind others).

    Subjects cannot be moved up/down, because the entire point of this tool is to compare their height. However, I am open to discussion if you have a case that would make such a feature useful.

  5. Saving the chart
    Sadly, there is no way to save the output at the moment. When you are finished, just zoom out (or in, or not at all) and take a screenshot.
